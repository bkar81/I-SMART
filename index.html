Current editor capabilities
  • Image-only zoom/pan and two-finger navigation
  • Hand, Select, Freehand, Highlighter, Line, Arrow, Rectangle, Oval, Text, Step, Blur and Pixelate
  • Tool-specific customization and editable Layer properties
  • Layers management with visibility, ordering, duplication and deletion
  • Standard/custom color picker with RGB, HSL and HEX
  • Global eyedropper and separate opacity controls
  • Grouped freehand strokes
  • Rectangle, Blur and Pixelate can be used for redaction without a separate Redact tool
  • Settings backup and restore through JSON
✏️ Pen & Freehand Drawing

I-SMART gives you control over how your drawing is organized into Layers.

Keep strokes together ON
Your entire drawing stays together as one Layer, even when you lift your finger between strokes. This is useful when writing or drawing something that you want to move or edit as a single object.

Keep strokes together OFF
Every time you lift your finger, that stroke becomes its own Layer. This is useful when you want to move, resize, recolor, or delete individual strokes separately.

Example:
If you write “I Love I-SMART” with several strokes and Keep strokes together is ON, the whole writing can be moved together. If it is OFF, each stroke can be handled independently.

Sharing / deployment

I-SMART is packaged as a standalone HTML application and can be hosted from a static site such as GitHub Pages. The service worker, manifest, icons and supporting legal/documentation files can be hosted alongside this HTML for the installable PWA deployment.

I-SMART is designed to work locally and does not require an ISMART-operated application server for normal image annotation.

Layers

Keep strokes together

When enabled, all your pen strokes stay together as one Layer until you choose another tool. You can lift your finger between strokes and continue drawing — I-SMART will keep them together so you can move, resize, or edit the entire drawing at once.

When disabled, each stroke becomes a separate Layer, so you can move or edit each stroke independently.
